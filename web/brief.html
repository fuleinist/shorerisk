Method

  1. Each asset is matched to the nearest shoreline transect. The inland distance is measured along that transect from the baseline shoreline.
  2. The setback at a given year is the retreat rate multiplied by the number of years. A negative rate means retreat.
  3. Bands: A is 0 to 20 years, B is 20 to 50, C is 50 to 100, and D is beyond 100 years or no retreat.
  4. The 95% confidence range uses the standard error of the rate. It sets the optimistic and pessimistic years.
  5. Confidence combines the transect data grade and the cross-shore offset of the asset.

Limits

  • The rate is historical. It is not a forecast.
  • The model assumes a constant rate. Storms, wave run-up and sea level acceleration are not included.
  • Transects are straight lines. They cover a 60 m wide corridor.
  • Costs are order-of-cost in 2026 AUD. An engineer must confirm them.
  • Rocky coasts, headlands and estuaries are outside the rate product.
  • This brief is a screening aid. It is not a coastal hazard assessment or a statutory plan.
